## Blackley properties

Blackley has a mix of older terraced housing, council-built properties from various periods, and more recent developments. The traditional terraces commonly have electrical systems that have been upgraded piece by piece over the years - the consumer unit may have been replaced at some point but the wiring behind it might not have been, and the earthing and bonding arrangements don't always reflect what current regulations require. We also come across a fair number of properties where someone's had a go at extending things themselves over the years, and the EICR inspection picks up what's been done properly and what hasn't.

The council-built stock and post-war housing varies. Higher Blackley and the areas around Heaton Park have their own mix, with larger semis and detached properties that sometimes have had extensions or loft conversions with electrical installation work of variable quality added on top of the original wiring.

## Common electrical issues in Blackley

The terraced stock commonly turns up electrical installations where the wiring has been extended over the decades without the consumer unit or the earthing being upgraded to cope. Consumer units that don't meet current safety standards, inadequate circuits for modern electrical loads, and earthing arrangements that need updating are all regular findings on an EICR in Blackley. We also see a fair number of installations where DIY work has been done - added spurs, extra sockets, sometimes even new circuits - without the installation being properly tested or certified afterwards. That's the kind of thing an EICR inspection is designed to pick up.

The council-built properties vary a fair bit. Some have been properly rewired with a modern installation, others still have the original wiring with additions that have accumulated over the decades. The EICR tells you which situation you're dealing with.

## For landlords

[Landlord law](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance) in Blackley works the same as elsewhere: an EICR every five years plus one for each new tenant - in plain terms, an electrical installation condition report kept current for every letting. Fines for letting it slip reach £30,000, and the local authority can have remedial installation work done and charge it back to you. Existing tenants must be given the report within 28 days, new tenants before they move in, and C1, C2 or FI findings come with 28 days to put them right. Ashley is NAPIT registered, so the EICR and the remedial work it identifies can both come from the same electrician.
