# Commercial Solar for Oldham Businesses

Commercial solar tends to outperform domestic for one straightforward reason: businesses are using the electricity during the day when the panels are generating it. There's no need for a battery to bridge to the evening - the energy gets used as it's produced, which means most of what you generate offsets what you'd otherwise be paying for. We model the payback from your own consumption rather than quoting an average.

We're based in [Prestwich](/prestwich/commercial-solar-installations/), about thirteen minutes down the road from Oldham.

## Oldham businesses where it tends to work

Oldham's industrial heritage means there are still plenty of factories and manufacturing units running heavy machinery. High electricity consumption from production equipment is what makes the payback fastest, and businesses spending £1,000+ a month tend to see the most transformation.

The warehouses and distribution units along the M60 corridor through Hollinwood and Chadderton have large flat roofs that are well-suited to commercial solar. Shops, restaurants and takeaways running commercial kitchens through the day benefit from the alignment between consumption and generation. Anything running refrigeration or cold storage continuously sits in the same category.

The borough also has a fair amount of converted mill buildings with large, unobstructed roof areas. Whether the roof is flat or pitched, those work well for commercial systems.

## Flat and pitched roofs

Flat roofs make our job easier - we mount the panels at the optimal angle regardless of building orientation. Pitched roofs on factories, warehouses and larger commercial buildings work too. If there's spare land or parking, ground-mounted systems can supplement the rooftop or power [EV charging points](/oldham/electric-vehicle-charger-installations/) for staff vehicles. We've installed setups where the staff charging runs entirely off the solar.

## Oldham Council and the green direction

Oldham declared a Climate Emergency in 2019 and became the UK's first "Green New Deal Council" in 2020. The council runs Oldham Community Power, which puts solar on local schools, and secured £20m in Levelling Up funding for green initiatives. The local culture is on the same page as commercial solar rather than against it.
