# Solar Panel Removal & Refit in Manchester

Solar panels usually come off because of the roof, not because of the panels. A re-roof, a roof repair, a dormer, a chimney, a flat roof replacement or storm damage can all mean the array has to come off before the roofing team can get at the covering, then go back on once the roof is sound again.

We remove and refit solar arrays across Greater Manchester and further afield. Ashley is an [MCS-certified](/accreditations/mcs-certified/) installer and a [NAPIT-registered](/accreditations/napit/) electrician, so the DC side, the isolation, the testing and the paperwork are handled by the same person who does the roof work coordination.

## When panels need to come off

The common reasons we see are:

- A full re-roof, whether that is slate, tile or a flat roof covering
- A roof repair around a chimney, valley, flashing or damaged section
- A dormer, loft conversion or extension that changes the roof layout
- A flat roof replacement where the mounting rails have to come up with the covering
- Storm damage or a failed fixing that needs the array lifted to put it right
- A roof that has reached the end of its life and is being replaced before the panels go back on

Removing panels is not a case of undoing a few brackets. The array has to be isolated safely on the DC side as well as the AC side, the panels have to be handled and stored without damage, and the roof has to be left watertight while the roofing work happens.

## How the job runs

Ashley surveys the array and the roof first. That means looking at the mounting system, the age and condition of the panels, the cabling and isolators, the roof covering, and how your roofer plans to work. Scaffolding and access are agreed before anything comes off.

The sequence is planned with you and the roofing team: what comes off, what can be reused, which fixings and flashings need replacing, where the panels are stored, and when the roofers need the roof clear. On the day, the DC and AC sides are isolated and labelled, the panels and rails come off, and the roof is left protected.

The roofers then do their work. Ashley works around their programme and checks the mounting points, flashings and cable route while the roof is open, because that is the point where a problem is easy to put right and expensive to discover later.

When the roof is ready, the array goes back on with new fixings or flashings where they are needed. The cabling is reconnected, the earthing is checked, and the system is tested and recommissioned: string tests, insulation resistance, earth continuity, inverter start-up and monitoring. The paperwork is updated before handover.

## What happens to the panels

The panels are checked before they go back on. If they are sound, they are reused. If a panel is cracked, delaminated or failing, it gets replaced rather than refitted and forgotten about. We will tell you before the job what can be reused and what cannot, and if a replacement panel is needed we check whether a compatible one is available.

If the panels themselves have failed, that is a different job from a removal and refit. We can replace failed panels and check the rest of the array at the same time, which is what happened on a job where three panels had failed and the customer wanted the system put right rather than patched up.

## Will the system still be certified?

The refit is tested and documented, including the array, the cabling and the earthing. Where the original system was MCS-certified, we keep the paperwork in order and provide the test results. If you claim export payments under the Smart Export Guarantee, check with your supplier that they have the updated details after the work.

The system also has to be safe to switch back on. That is why the testing and recommissioning are part of the job rather than an optional extra. A roof that looks finished is not the same as an array that has been tested and is ready to generate again.

## What it costs

There is no fixed price. The job depends on the size of the array, the roof access and scaffolding, the condition of the mounting rails and fixings, whether the roofers or we provide the scaffold, how many visits the roof programme needs, and whether any panels or parts have to be replaced.

Ashley surveys the property and gives you a written quote with the scope set out. If the roofers find something unexpected once the covering is off, you get told before any extra work is done.
