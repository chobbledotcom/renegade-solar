# Consumer Unit & Fuse Board Upgrades in Manchester

A consumer unit is the box that splits the incoming electricity supply into the circuits around the house. Most people still call it the fuse box or the fuse board. It holds the protective devices that stop a fault turning into a fire or a shock, and when it is old, damaged or full, replacing it is often the sensible repair rather than a full rewire.

## When a consumer unit needs replacing

An old board is not automatically a bad board. Plenty of houses have a consumer unit that is dated but still doing its job, and replacing it would be spending money for the sake of a newer lid. The cases where replacement usually makes sense are:

- The board has rewireable fuses or old cartridge fuses and no RCD protection
- There are signs of overheating, scorching, a burning smell, cracked casing or moisture inside
- There are no spare ways left for an extension, electric shower, solar panels, a battery or an EV charger
- An [EICR](/services/electrical-safety-inspections-eicr/) has coded the board or its protective devices as C2
- Parts are obsolete or the board cannot be isolated safely
- Previous work has left a mix of circuits that nobody can identify or test properly

If you are not sure which side of that line you are on, an EICR is the proper way to find out. It tests the installation circuit by circuit and gives you a report you can act on. A replacement can follow if it is needed, but the report comes first.

## What a consumer unit replacement involves

Ashley surveys the property first. That means looking at the incoming supply, the main fuse rating, the earthing and main protective bonding, the circuits already in the board, the cable routes and what you want to add. The survey is free, and you get a written quote that sets out the board, the protective devices, any earthing or bonding work and the testing.

On the day, the supply is isolated and the circuits are labelled before the old board comes out. The new consumer unit goes in with individual circuit protection, the earthing and bonding are brought up to standard, and surge protection is fitted where the regulations require it. Spare ways are left where solar, a battery or an EV charger is planned, so the next job does not mean changing the board again.

The installation is then tested circuit by circuit: continuity, insulation resistance, polarity, earth fault loop impedance and RCD or RCBO operation. You get an Electrical Installation Certificate, a circuit chart and labels that match the new board, and the work is certified through our NAPIT registration.

## Consumer units for solar, batteries and EV chargers

A lot of our consumer unit work comes out of the renewable side of the business. Solar panels, a home battery and an EV charger all need circuits, protection and spare capacity, and the existing board is often where the constraint sits.

Sometimes the existing unit has room and the right protection, and nothing needs changing. Sometimes it needs a larger board, an extra way or an upgrade to the earthing before the new equipment can go in. The survey works that out before anything is ordered. If the supply needs a DNO application for a larger solar system, that is handled alongside the electrical work - our [G98 and G99 guide](/g98-g99-dno-applications-solar-manchester/) explains when that applies.

The same logic applies to [home battery installations](/services/home-battery-installations/) and [EV charger installation](/services/electric-vehicle-charger-installations/). The charger or battery is only as good as the supply and protection behind it, which is why the consumer unit gets checked as part of those jobs rather than treated as someone else's problem.

## What a consumer unit replacement costs

There is no fixed price, because the job changes with the number of circuits, the state of the earthing and bonding, access around the board, and whether testing turns up a circuit that needs remedial work. A straight board swap in a house with a sound installation is a different job from an older property where the earthing needs bringing up to standard at the same time.

Ashley surveys the property and gives you a written quote with the scope set out. If something unexpected turns up during the work, you get told before it is done rather than finding it on the invoice.
