## EICR for rental properties - £150 plus VAT, same day certificates

**Fixed price of £150 plus VAT for any domestic property** - it doesn't matter whether it's a one-bed flat or a five-bed HMO, the price is the same. You get your inspection and all certificates on the same day.

The legal position for landlords is straightforward:

- **EICR every 5 years**, plus one for each new tenant
- **Existing tenants** must have the report within 28 days
- **New tenants** must have the report before they move in
- **C1, C2 or FI issues** must be fixed within 28 days
- **HMOs** are subject to the same 5-year cycle

Penalties for non-compliance are serious - **fines up to £30,000**, plus local authorities can arrange remedial work and bill you for it. Tenancy deposit schemes can also get involved.

## What we check

A proper EICR isn't a quick visual - we test everything that needs testing:

**Visual inspection** covers wiring, switches, sockets and the consumer unit. We're looking for damage, signs of overheating, and general wear.

**Electrical testing** is where the technical work happens - continuity testing, insulation resistance, earth fault loop impedance, and RCD testing to ensure those trip switches work when they're needed.

You get a detailed report and all certificates on the same day. We explain results in plain English - C1 means immediate danger, C2 is potentially dangerous, C3 is "could be better but not dangerous", and FI means we need to investigate further.

## Older rental stock - what to expect

Many rental properties in North Manchester are older. Victorian terraces (common in Prestwich and Whitefield) often have:

- Old wiring systems like cloth-covered cables
- Inadequate earthing - proper earthing wasn't standard when these houses were built
- Circuits designed for gas lighting and a few basic electrical items, not modern loads
- Ancient fuse boxes with no RCD protection

It's worth bearing in mind that **70% of electrical fires happen in buildings built before 1950**. A proper EICR matters more for older stock, not less.

New builds aren't automatically safe either - **1 in 5 new builds have electrical faults** including loose connections, missing earth bonding, and downlight earth wires that have been cut off rather than connected properly.
