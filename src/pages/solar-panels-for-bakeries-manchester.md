## Solar for bakeries, cafes and food businesses

Commercial kitchens and refrigeration are some of the most electricity-hungry operations a small business can run. Ovens, proving cabinets, walk-in chillers, freezers and prep equipment all draw power throughout the working day - exactly when solar panels generate at their peak.

We recently spoke to a [Prestwich](/services/commercial-solar-installations/prestwich/) baker spending **£10,000 a month** on electricity who nearly went under because of it. That's the kind of bill a properly designed [commercial solar installation](/services/commercial-solar-installations/) can transform. Most commercial installs reduce monthly costs by 70-100%, with payback typically in three to four years and 20+ years of low-cost energy after that.

## Why food businesses see strong returns

The financial case for solar on a bakery, cafe or restaurant is stronger than for a typical home because your demand and your generation line up. Bakeries are running ovens early in the morning. Cafes and restaurants are using prep equipment, dishwashers and refrigeration through lunch and dinner service. Refrigeration and cold storage are pulling power 24/7 with peaks during the day. Solar panels generate from daylight, not just direct sunshine, so even in our northern climate they produce useful power across the working day.

Because you're using nearly every kilowatt-hour your panels produce as it's generated, you don't need [battery storage](/services/home-battery-installations/) to make the numbers work the way a domestic property does. That dramatically improves the return on investment.

## What a system might look like for your business

There's no one-size-fits-all answer - that's the point of the survey. When you contact us, a qualified electrician assesses your property and reviews your electricity usage. Not a salesperson working from a script. We design systems based on your actual roof, your actual usage and your actual budget, then we present the options.

For most commercial properties we'll look at:

- **Flat roofs** - which give us flexibility to point panels south regardless of how the building sits, ideal for retail units and warehouses with large unused roof areas
- **Pitched roofs** - which work well on factory units, larger commercial buildings and hospitality properties
- **Ground-mounted systems** - if you've got spare land, grass verges or yard space

We'll often present multiple options. A recent design for a [Bolton](/services/commercial-solar-installations/bolton/) carpet warehouse, for example, included a 50-panel flat-roof option giving three-year payback and a 115-panel option covering both flat and pitched roofs with four-year payback.

## What you need to qualify

The main requirement is that **you own the building** - solar installations on leased or rented food premises don't work because you need long-term ownership to realise the payback. You'll also need sufficient roof space or land, and ideally high electricity consumption. The more you're spending now, the faster the payback.

If you're a tenant in a leased unit, it may still be worth speaking to your landlord - we can put together the financial case for them as the asset owner.
