Bakeries, cafes and restaurants tend to be owner-operated. The owner is in the kitchen at 4am or running service at lunchtime. There's usually no facilities team, no time for a sales presentation, and the last thing anyone wants is multiple contractors pointing at each other when something doesn't work.

## Why food businesses see strong returns

The financial case for solar on a bakery, cafe or restaurant is stronger than for a typical home because your demand and your generation line up. Bakeries are running ovens early in the morning. Cafes and restaurants are pulling power through lunch and dinner service. Refrigeration and cold storage run continuously, with peaks during the day. Solar panels generate from daylight, not just direct sunshine, so even in our northern climate they produce useful power across the working day.

Because you're using nearly every kilowatt-hour your panels produce as it's generated, you don't need [battery storage](/services/home-battery-installations/) to make the numbers work the way a domestic property does, which dramatically improves the return.

## What a system might look like for your business

There's no one-size-fits-all answer, which is the point of doing the survey properly. Ashley will look at:

- [Flat roofs](/flat-roof-commercial-solar-manchester/), which give the flexibility to point panels south regardless of how the building sits
- Pitched roofs, which work well on factory units, larger commercial buildings and hospitality properties
- [Ground-mounted systems](/ground-mounted-solar-installations-manchester/), if you have spare land, grass verges or yard space

He'll often present multiple options. A recent design for a [Bolton](/bolton/commercial-solar-installations/) carpet warehouse, for example, included a 50-panel flat-roof option and a larger 115-panel option covering both the flat and pitched roofs. The principle is the same for a bakery, just sized to match your kitchen's load.

If the building is smaller and the useful roof space is on an extension, store room or garage-style outbuilding, the [flat roof property page](/properties/flat-roofs/) explains the domestic and small-building side of that work.

## What you need to qualify

The main requirement is that you own the building. Solar installations on leased food premises don't work because you need long-term ownership for the payback to be worth it. You'll also need sufficient roof space or land and ideally high electricity consumption, since the more you're spending now the faster the payback.

If you're a tenant in a leased unit, it may still be worth speaking to your landlord. Ashley can put together the financial case for them as the asset owner.
