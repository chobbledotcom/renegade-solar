## Flat roof solar for commercial properties

A flat roof on a commercial building is one of the best places in the UK to put solar panels. Flat roofs give us flexibility that pitched roofs don't - we can mount panels pointing south regardless of how the building is oriented. That's why retail units, warehouses, factories and commercial properties with large flat roofs generate substantial energy throughout the day.

Many commercial buildings have large flat roof spaces going unused. We can turn those into assets that produce savings for decades.

## A real flat-roof example

We're currently designing a system for a carpet and interior design warehouse in [Bolton](/services/commercial-solar-installations/bolton/) with a massive flat roof. They spend **£700 a month** on electricity. Our 50-panel flat-roof design takes that to zero with a three-year payback. A larger 115-panel option, covering both the flat roof and the pitched sections, pays back in four years.

That's the pattern: **most commercial installations reduce monthly costs by 70-100%, with payback typically within 3-4 years**, and 20+ years of low-cost energy after that.

## Why flat roofs work brilliantly

The advantages compared to pitched roofs:

- **South-facing regardless of building orientation** - we mount panels on a tilted frame at the optimum angle, so a building that runs east-west still produces like one that runs north-south
- **Easier system design** - large unobstructed flat roofs can take bigger arrays
- **Easier access** for installation and any future maintenance
- **Less visual impact** - panels on a flat roof are typically not visible from ground level

For [commercial solar](/services/commercial-solar-installations/) on the right building, that combination is hard to beat.

## What kinds of buildings work

We typically design flat-roof commercial systems for:

- **Warehouses and distribution buildings** - usually owner-occupied, large roofs, daytime electricity use
- **Retail units** - cash-and-carry, furniture stores, carpet warehouses, supermarkets
- **Factories and industrial units** - especially those with three-phase supplies and high consumption
- **Hotels and hospitality** - kitchens, heating and laundry running through the day
- **Offices** - where the building is owner-occupied

## What about pitched roofs?

If you have pitched roofs as well, those can usually be incorporated. The Bolton job is a good example - the larger design uses both flat and pitched sections to maximise generation. Pitched roofs still work well, particularly on factories, hotels (Premier Inns are a good example), or larger commercial buildings.

## What you need to qualify

The main requirement is that **you own the building** - solar installations on leased properties don't work because you need long-term ownership to realise the payback. You'll also need:

- Sufficient roof load capacity (we assess this as part of the survey)
- High enough electricity consumption to give a strong return - the more you spend, the faster the payback
- A plan to keep the property long-term to maximise the financial benefits

Because commercial properties consume electricity during the day when [solar generation](/services/solar-and-battery-installations/) is at its peak, you use nearly every kilowatt-hour your panels produce - so you don't need [battery storage](/services/home-battery-installations/) to make the numbers work. That dramatically improves the return on investment.
