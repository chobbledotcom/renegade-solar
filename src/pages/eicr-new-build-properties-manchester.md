## What we actually check

A proper EICR isn't a quick visual:

**Visual inspection** covers all your wiring, switches, sockets, and consumer unit condition. We're looking for signs of damage, overheating, or general wear, plus checking whether everything still looks fit for purpose.

**Electrical testing** is where the proper technical work happens:

- **Continuity testing** - circuits are properly connected
- **Insulation resistance testing** - wires aren't going to give you a shock
- **Earth fault loop impedance testing** - safety systems work properly
- **RCD testing** - those trip switches that protect you actually work when they're needed

You get a detailed report on the same day, explaining what we found in plain English.

## What we typically find on new builds

The faults we see on new-build properties are usually a result of corners cut during a competitive build phase rather than fundamental problems with the design. Common ones:

- **Loose connections** that weren't properly tightened during installation
- **Missing earth bonding** in bathrooms and kitchens
- **Downlights with cut-off earth wires** - the earth conductor is supposed to be connected, not snipped
- **RCD arrangements** that don't fully comply with current regulations
- **Installation shortcuts** from rushed construction phases

**Modern developments and conversions** can have any or all of these. The EICR catches them.

## Why have an independent EICR if the builder gave me a cert?

The EIC (Electrical Installation Certificate) you get from the builder is the installer self-certifying their own work. An independent EICR is a third-party check by someone who has nothing to gain from the result.

Common reasons buyers and homeowners want one:

- **Peace of mind** - especially if the property is your biggest financial commitment
- **NHBC or warranty issues** - documented evidence of any faults found
- **Snagging** - electrical issues are easier to argue with the developer when there's a NAPIT-registered electrician's report behind them
- **Selling on later** - buyers and solicitors increasingly ask for an EICR
