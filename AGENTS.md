# Renegade Solar Site Guide

This is the single source of truth for writing and editing the Renegade Solar website. It combines company facts, E-E-A-T evidence, SEO requirements, location-page guidance, and house voice.

## Priorities

1. Rank for the work Renegade Solar does and bring in suitable enquiries.
2. Keep every factual claim accurate, supportable, and current.
3. Show first-hand experience through real jobs, property details, photographs, reviews, accreditations, and named places.
4. Sound like Ashley rather than a marketing agency, without sacrificing keywords or clarity.

If these priorities conflict, factual accuracy comes first, SEO coverage second, and voice third.

## SEO Hard Rules

The site is a marketing site. Voice serves search ranking; it never overrides it.

1. A rewrite must never reduce keyword coverage. Before editing a page, list its service terms, brands, products, and place names. Their frequency must not fall.
2. Never shorten an existing page to apply the voice. The revised word count must meet or exceed the original. Location and landing pages under roughly 250 words are too thin.
3. Protect the `title`, `description`, headings, link text, and first paragraph. Keep their target keywords.
4. Keep explicit service language. "Solar panel installation," "battery storage," and "EV charger installer" cannot be replaced by vague phrases such as "what we do."
5. Every location page must use the relevant place name naturally in the title, headings, opening paragraph, body, and internal links.

Target terms include, but are not limited to:

- solar panel installation / installer
- battery installation / battery storage
- home battery installation
- EV charger installation / installer
- EICR / electrical safety inspection
- electrician / electrical installation
- MCS-certified
- renewable energy
- commercial solar
- solar panels plus the relevant location

## E-E-A-T Rules

### Prefer Primary Evidence

Use evidence in this order:

1. Current certifications, registration pages, contracts, commissioning records, and first-party project documents.
2. Current verified reviews in `src/_data/reviews.json` with source attribution.
3. Project photographs and specific first-party posts in `social-posts/` and `instagram-posts/`.
4. Manufacturer documentation and official government or industry sources.
5. Reputable secondary sources.

Never invent a testimonial, project location, customer, system size, product, saving, or performance result.

### Show Experience, Do Not Merely Claim It

Strong E-E-A-T details include:

- the property type and roof material
- obstructions such as rooflights, chimneys, dormers, parapets, and trees
- whether the array is on-roof, in-roof, flat-roof, or ground-mounted
- actual panel, inverter, battery, or charger models
- system size and battery capacity
- survey, design, electrical, and commissioning considerations
- the named location when confirmed
- real installation photographs with descriptive alt text
- a relevant attributed customer review

### Claims Requiring Qualification Or A Source

Do not present the following as universal facts without a current credible source and the assumptions behind the number:

- electricity-bill savings of 50%, 70%, or 80%
- property-value increases of 3% to 4%
- annual carbon savings of 1.3 to 1.5 tonnes
- cloudy-day efficiency percentages
- exact payback periods
- guaranteed Smart Export Guarantee income
- complete protection from power cuts
- "the only MCS installer" in an area
- zero bills, 100% self-sufficiency, or guaranteed future energy costs

Use modelled estimates for the customer's actual property and tariff where possible.

### Known Misinterpretations To Avoid

- `social-posts/2026-02-09T17-49-54Z-916229350936460.json` promotes Channel 4's *Guy Martin's House Without Bills*. It is not evidence that Renegade Solar or Ashley appeared on Channel 4.
- Cross-posted Facebook and Instagram records are one source, not independent corroboration.
- An accreditation logo in a promotional image supports further verification; it is not a substitute for checking the current registration.
- A social caption offering a service proves that it was marketed, not necessarily that the pictured project used that service.
- Personal posts and unrelated marketplace posts must not be used as business evidence.

## Common Concerns

### Suitability

- Address the actual property type.
- Explain options for awkward orientations, roof obstructions, flat roofs, ground mounting, or battery-only systems.
- Use the summer 2025 rooflight and flat-roof portfolio as evidence of design around real constraints, without inventing locations or specifications.

### Installation Time

- A standard residential installation commonly takes one to two days.
- Qualify this for scaffolding, roof repairs, consumer-unit work, weather, DNO requirements, and non-standard mounting.

### Planning

- Many domestic installations are permitted development.
- Listed buildings, conservation areas, flat roofs, ground arrays, and unusual positions can require additional checks or consent.
- Do not promise planning approval.

### Cost And Benefit

- Discuss energy usage, daytime demand, roof yield, tariff, export rate, battery strategy, and expected equipment life.
- Use project-specific estimates rather than generic guaranteed savings.
- Explain protection from energy-price rises as reduced grid dependence, not immunity from future costs.

## Battery-Only Location Guidance

- Apartments and dense urban areas may have limited roof rights; discuss ownership, landlord, freeholder, lease, and management-company constraints.
- Conservation areas and historical properties may suit an internal battery where visible solar panels are constrained, subject to electrical and fire-safety requirements.
- Shaded homes can use off-peak battery charging without solar.

## Write For Customers, Not Researchers

Repository evidence, claim checks, source limitations, and editorial reasoning are internal tools. They must shape the page, but they must not become the page. Website visitors are homeowners, landlords, and businesses trying to decide whether Renegade can help with their property.

- Describe the job, property, problem, work, and result directly. Do not tell the reader that a photograph or post is "evidence" of them.
- Keep research language in `AGENTS.md`, source records, or internal notes. Avoid public phrases such as "the source supports," "the photographs establish," "this is not being presented as," "useful evidence," and "we have not repeated the unsupported claim."
- State only the supported fact instead of publishing the rejected alternative. If Renegade completed the electrical installation but not the later solar phase, write what the electrical phase included and say solar was scheduled for later where that context is useful. Do not narrate the fact-checking process.
- Explain qualifications in terms the customer needs. Battery location, access, structural checks, planning, and system compatibility are useful. A discussion of why an internal source cannot prove a claim is not.
- Case studies should read as accounts of real work: what the customer or property needed, what Ashley and the team did, what decisions mattered, and how the finished installation works.
- Review attribution, dates, accreditations, specifications, and project locations can appear where they help the reader judge relevance or trustworthiness. They should not read like research citations pasted into prose.
- Before publishing, ask: "Would a prospective customer care about this sentence, or is it explaining our editorial process?" Remove the latter from the public page.

## House Voice

The voice is dry, plain-spoken, sceptical of marketing language, and recognisably Mancunian without dialect cosplay. It should sound like a Manchester electrician writing about his own work, not a copywriter writing a northern character.

This is guidance, not a template. Repeating the same rhetorical pattern makes the site sound generated.

### The Main Trap

Spoken humour relies on fragments, comic lists, punchlines, and timing. Lift those patterns into website copy and they become advertising copy. Mancunian character should come from real places, jobs, brands, materials, property quirks, and occasional natural word choices, not a performed accent.

### The WhatsApp Test

Read the sentence aloud. Could Ashley type it on his phone in the back of a van? If it has a polished slogan structure, take the polish off while keeping every keyword.

### Voice References

Do not imitate any one person. The shared plain-speaking DNA is useful:

- Mark E Smith
- John Cooper Clarke
- Caroline Aherne
- Maxine Peake
- Shaun Ryder
- Peter Kay
- Sean Dyche
- Fred Dibnah, especially for craft and material specificity
- John Prescott
- ordinary dialogue in *Coronation Street*

These people are references for spoken directness, not a source of lines or punchlines.

### Voice Principles

1. Use first person where it is honest. "I run Renegade Solar, fitting solar panel installations across North Manchester" keeps both voice and keyword. Use "we" naturally for the in-house team.
2. Prefer specifics to abstractions. Write "a flat roof around a roof lantern" rather than "challenging environments."
3. Use plain words. "A few quid," "a fair bit," "hold up well," and "on the tools" can work when natural, but one or two such phrases per page is enough.
4. Allow some looseness. Natural sub-clauses and occasional hedges sound more credible than polished parallel slogans.
5. Use complete sentences in prose. Avoid fragments such as "The lot," "No messing," and "Same-day certificates."
6. Trust the reader. Do not explain a joke, justify every sentence, or repeat the conclusion.

Keywords such as "renewable energy" and "MCS-certified installer" stay even if they are not pub language.

### Patterns To Avoid

- Fragment closers: "The lot." or "No hard sell."
- Cinematic summaries: "Renegade Solar followed." or "And the rest is history."
- Polished rule-of-three slogans.
- A comic third item added to a list.
- "Not X, not Y, not Z - Ashley."
- "And yes, it works up here."
- Repeated tag questions.
- Deliberately trying to be funny.
- Generic northern markers used instead of real local detail.
- Em dashes or double hyphens. Use a single spaced hyphen for an ordinary aside.

Bullets and headings do not need to be complete sentences.

### No-Go Words And Phrases

Avoid these outside direct quotations:

- owt / nowt
- our kid
- champion as an adjective
- ay up / by 'eck / ee bah gum
- proper or dead as generic intensifiers
- me in place of my
- phonetic accent spelling
- honest as the day is long
- salt of the earth
- no nonsense

### Editing Process

1. Record the current word count and target-keyword counts.
2. Write or edit in plain English while preserving every keyword.
3. Read it aloud and mark brochure-like sentences.
4. Apply the WhatsApp test without deleting keywords or useful detail.
5. Check every factual claim against repository evidence or a current external source.
6. Recount words and keywords. Neither may fall below the original page.
7. Check headings, metadata, internal links, alt text, and location references.

### Where Voice Applies

- About-page copy
- Homepage section introductions, but not at the expense of the SEO-heavy hero
- Transitions and supporting paragraphs
- Headings only where target terms remain protected elsewhere and in the heading set
- Link text, while preserving descriptive keyword-rich anchors

Do not apply the informal voice to legal policies, technical specifications, accreditation details, contact data, form labels, or anywhere ambiguity could cost the reader money or safety.

## Page Motifs

Pages are broken up with reusable snippets rather than long unbroken slabs of prose. Every one lives in `src/_includes/motifs/` and is dropped in with `{% include "motifs/<name>.html" %}`. Each file starts with a comment listing the variables it accepts, and clears them afterwards so a second use on the same page does not inherit the first one's settings.

| Snippet | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `section-head.html` | Eyebrow pill, heading, standfirst. Used inside the other motifs. |
| `trust-bar.html` | Horizontal run of short benefit statements with tick marks. |
| `stat-strip.html` | Four headline numbers, pulled live from `reviews.json`. |
| `cta-banner.html` | Full-width "get in touch" banner with two buttons. |
| `process.html` | The six numbered steps from first phone call to handover. |
| `faq.html` | `<details>` accordion. A page can override the questions with a `faqs` list in its front matter. |
| `why-us.html` | Four cards explaining what a small in-house outfit changes. |
| `quote-feature.html` | One real review, blown up on a dark band. |
| `review-strip.html` | Three review cards with a link to the full list. |
| `photo-strip.html` | Sideways-scrolling run of real job photographs, filtered by `gallery_tags`. |
| `feature-split.html` | Photograph one side, words the other. |
| `service-tiles.html` | The services as picture cards. |
| `areas-strip.html` | Every location page as a chip. |
| `page-hero.html` | Dark header with breadcrumb, page title and the short benefit run. |
| `contact-panel.html` | The contact form with reasons to fill it in beside it. |
| `accreditation-band.html` | The accreditation logos with a line of context. |

Copy for the data-driven motifs lives in `src/_data/motifs.json` - benefits, the process steps, the FAQs and the contact points. Change it there and it changes on every page at once. Anything factual in that file is subject to the same evidence rules as page copy.

Steps and questions are grouped into named sets, because an EICR is not a roof job and solar questions are no use on an inspection page. A page picks its set in front matter:

```yaml
process_set: inspection   # solar (default) | battery | commercial | ev | inspection | none
faq_set: inspection       # solar (default) | inspection | ev | none
```

A page can also supply its own `faqs` list of `{q, a}` pairs, which wins over the set.

Two other front matter fields matter:

- `hero_sub` is the standfirst shown under the page title in the hero. The hero does **not** fall back to the meta `description`, deliberately: descriptions are written for search results and several carry claims - "3-4 year payback", "bills to zero" - that the rules above do not allow as on-page copy. A page with no `hero_sub` just shows its title.
- `photo` is the photograph used behind the page hero and on the service tile.

Every credential a motif states is linked to the page that backs it up - the MCS number to the MCS accreditation page, the review score to `/reviews/`. When you add a claim to a motif, add the link with it.

Layouts apply the motifs automatically, so a new markdown page gets the full treatment without doing anything. Three Eleventy filters make that work:

- `firstHeading` and `withoutFirstHeading` lift a page's `<h1>` out of the rendered markdown so the page hero can show it without the heading appearing twice.
- `splitContent` chops long markdown at its own `<h2>` boundaries so a layout can drop a motif between the sections. It refuses to split inside an unclosed `<div>`, and returns a single chunk for short pages.

Styling is in `src/css/_motifs.scss`, with colours, radii and shadows in `src/css/_tokens.scss`. A full-bleed stripe is a `.band` with a `.band__inner` inside it; alternate `band--white`, `band--dark`, `band--green` and `band--green-tint` down a page to keep it moving.

## Final Check

Every page should be locally specific, keyword-complete, factually supportable, useful to a prospective customer, and recognisably written by a working Manchester electrician. If a claim sounds impressive but the evidence does not support it, leave it out or ask.
